\newcommand{\textTemporalConvergence}{%
To isolate the temporal discretization error, we fix a fine spatial mesh
($N_x = 256$, domain $[-3,3]$) and vary only the number of time steps.
Table~\ref{tab:temporal-clean} and Fig.~\ref{fig:temporal-clean} show
first-order convergence in time, consistent with the backward Euler
discretization, until the error reaches the spatial discretization floor.
The pre-plateau temporal convergence rate is approximately $0.8504$ for $\sigma=0.20$.
For $\sigma=0.05$ (convection-dominated regime), the temporal error constant
is smaller than the spatial discretization floor at this mesh resolution,
so first-order temporal convergence cannot be demonstrated independently of
the spatial error in this regime.
}

\newcommand{\textGammaRecovery}{%
While the ultraweak formulation provides a direct approximation of the log-price derivative $\vartheta_h \approx \partial_x u$ and hence an efficient Delta approximation, Gamma requires differentiating this derivative variable. Differentiating the within-element polynomial slope of the $L^2$ trial variable $\vartheta_h$ (method Raw) shows irregular, non-monotone convergence across mesh levels, indicating sensitivity to how the DPG solve distributes element DOFs, and produces larger absolute errors at each mesh size than the reconstruction methods (Table~\ref{tab:gamma-reconstruction}, mean EOC $\approx 1.27$).
We report a locally reconstructed Gamma obtained from polynomial recovery of element-average $\vartheta_h$ values using an over-determined least-squares fit over neighboring cells. LSQ-$P_2$ (9-cell stencil, 3 unknowns) achieves monotone EOC $\approx 1.64$, LSQ-$P_3$ (13-cell stencil, 4 unknowns) achieves EOC $\approx 1.65$, and a non-overlapping piecewise-$P_2$ macro-element projection ($L^2$-Proj-$P_2$) achieves mean EOC $\approx 1.60$.
The consistent improvement of LSQ over the raw element slope confirms that smoothing element-average $\vartheta_h$ values before differentiation delivers more reliable and higher-order Gamma estimates than direct within-element differentiation.
}
